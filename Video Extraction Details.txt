### Extraction of camera telemetry

The extractor `02_extract/extractTelemetry.py` reads original MP4 files and connects to a decoder (`02_extract/decorders.py`) for each camera. Records are sorted by timestamp, clip, and sample index before writing the camera-specific CSV.

Garmin. The decoder reads GPS records from a private UUID atom in the MP4 container. Twenty-byte records contain a timestamp, coordinate integers, and an encoded camera-reported speed. The timestamp is interpreted relative to the QuickTime epoch of 1 January 1904. Signed coordinate integers are scaled by 360/2^32 to obtain decimal degrees. A sentinel value identifies absent fixes. This extraction produces GPS and speed fields, without  accelerometer or gyroscope output.

REDTIGER. The decoder uses an MP4 GPS index to locate [Novatek freeGPS](citation) records. Initially extracted records of latitude and longitude are converted from degrees and minute values to decimal degrees, with hemisphere indicators determining sign. Calendar fields supply the UTC timestamp, an active/void marker supplies fix status, and a camera speed field supplies miles per hour. X and Y acceleration are reported as integer values between 1 and 3 -- presumably to indicate a low, medium, or high gravitational force. Multiplication of these integers by 0.048 g brings these values into a range that is comparable to the acceleration records of the other dashcams. No gravity or baseline calibration is applied.

Rove. The decoder extracts records in FFprobe format. FFprobe identifies SStarMeta (ssmd) data tracks, and FFmpeg extracts their payloads. Thirty two byte GPS records encode degrees and minute coordinates, calendar fields, fix sentinels, and a speed byte. Approximately 16.6 Hz three-axis acceleration samples are distributed across GPS records according to their relative sample counts and averaged into each GPS observation window. The original data contains multiple acceleration records per GPS reading. The average of the acceleration readings was grouped with the corresponding GPS reading.

GoPro. ExifTool extracts GPS timestamps, latitude, longitude, reported speed, and fix mode from the embedded GoPro metadata. The current pipeline labels the extracted speed values as kilometres per hour and retains them in that unit in the single camera CSV; these are the extraction tool's reported values, not a statement that every underlying GPMF speed field is stored in that unit. GoPro's metadata format is documented by the manufacturer, and the extraction tags are described by ExifTool.

GoPro GPS observations are retained at their nominal 10 Hz rate. Approximately 200 Hz acceleration and angular sequences are partitioned into GPS windows using the ratio of sequence lengths, then averaged within each window. Inertial values are expressed in the camera coordinate frame. The parser replaces non- or excessively large inertial tokens with zero before forming triplets. Both 2D and 3D GoPro fixes are accepted for the horizontal-position comparison

Additional parser paragraph removed from the descriptor on 2026-11-02 (verbatim):

The older GoPro parser substituted zero for nonfinite or absolute values above 1000 before averaging. Future extraction now rejects those tokens and incomplete triplets instead of silently inventing zero measurements. This guard does not retrospectively prove that the preserved CSVs contain no substituted values; that would require a full comparison with the original extraction output.
